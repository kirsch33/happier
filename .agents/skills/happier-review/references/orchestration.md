# Review Delegation

Delegation is optional. The primary reviewer owns scope, evidence assessment, finding verification, consolidation, and the final judgment. Keep the review with one owner unless the user, approved plan, or a concrete independent risk question explicitly calls for a separate lane.

## When to delegate

Delegate only a complete, independently checkable responsibility whose result can materially improve the review. Do not delegate mechanical inventories, dependent conclusions, duplicate rereads, or work merely to increase parallelism. State the question, the evidence that could falsify it, the affected corridor, and the deciding check before dispatch.

## Lane brief

Give a delegated reviewer the exact target and revision basis, current dirty-state warning, relevant paths and canonical-owner hypothesis, observed evidence, scope boundaries, success claim, deciding checks, write authority, and stop conditions. Tell every worker that concurrent work may exist and unrelated changes must be preserved. Use minimal inherited context by default; include more only when the decision cannot be expressed in the brief.

## Integration

Treat a lane result as a claim. Re-derive material findings from primary evidence and reconcile only the changed affected corridor. A read-only reviewer never fixes unless its brief explicitly grants that responsibility. Require an independent reviewer for release, security, schema/data, compatibility, or user-visible ship gates when available.

Stop when the requested scope and named risk seams have deciding evidence, findings are triaged, and residual gaps are explicit. Do not seek additional lanes for consensus or reassurance.
